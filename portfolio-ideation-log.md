## Wednesday, August 19, 2026

### Asset production

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000001 -->

**Direction — Increasing control over generated assets**

Use AI heavily for visual ideation while progressively taking more control over the final assets. Generated images can reveal possibilities quickly, but the generator's defaults should not become the portfolio's visual identity.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000002 -->

**Experiment — Multi-tool visual exploration**

Use access to Google AI Pro through November to push visual exploration, with Gemini generating possibilities, ChatGPT developing prompts, questioning concepts, and finding principles among the references, and other tools joining as useful. Look for spatial relationships, environmental storytelling, unusual mechanical objects, restrained theatrical staging, and places that provoke curiosity, without treating generated imagery as production-ready.

### Audio

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000003 -->

**Exploration — Sparse diegetic sound**

Favor sparse diegetic sound over a continuous soundtrack: wind, water against stone, a chime, or a mechanical noise somewhere beyond the visible scene.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000004 -->

**Exploration — Silence as part of the world**

Sound should be allowed to disappear. Silence matters, and making every object emit a sound when the pointer approaches would turn the world into an interface demonstration.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000005 -->

**Exploration — Sound on independent clocks**

Let sounds live on different clocks, including sounds without an obvious visible cause. A small amount of unexplained audio may imply a much larger world.

### Environment

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000006 -->

**Exploration — Environmental variation between visits**

Let visits vary through morning and evening, weather, tide or water state, fog, wind, distant machinery, and occasional chimes. The point is not to simulate a complete climate system, but to suggest that the place continues to exist when nobody is looking at it.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000007 -->

**Open Question — Real-time or real-weather states**

Should environmental states follow real time or real weather?

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000008 -->

**Open Question — Authored or probabilistic variation**

Should some environmental variation be authored, probabilistic, or a mixture of both?

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000009 -->

**Open Question — Meaning of the tide state**

Does a tide state need to correspond to anything real?

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000010 -->

**Open Question — Enough variation to create memory**

How much variation is enough to create memory without making the portfolio feel inconsistent?

### Interaction

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000011 -->

**Principle — Reward attention without demanding it**

Reward attention without training visitors to mouse over everything in search of animation. A place can occasionally reveal itself without making every detail advertise that it is interactive.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000012 -->

**Exploration — Repeat visits without game mechanics**

Environmental variation could reward repeat visits and create memory without relying on collectibles, achievements, or other explicit game mechanics.

### Motion

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000013 -->

**Reference — Theatrical staging and transitions**

*Darkest Dungeon* is useful for staging and transitions that feel theatrical without requiring everything on screen to move continuously.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000014 -->

**Principle — Alive, not animated**

Alive, not animated. Stillness should be the default state, and motion should be an event rather than a constant demonstration that the website can move.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000015 -->

**Exploration — Sparse and varied movement**

Use sparse, varied movement: a flag changing silhouette, a page lifting slightly, a chime swinging, water shifting between a few states, a crab appearing and disappearing, a distant wheel taking two minutes to turn, or fog moving almost imperceptibly. The interesting response may be, "Wait. Did that just move?"

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000016 -->

**Principle — Different things on different clocks**

Different things should live on different clocks. Water, people, machinery, weather, creatures, and sound need not move together or wait for the visitor to trigger them.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000017 -->

**Exploration — Events visitors may not see**

Some events might happen every visit, some only occasionally, and some so slowly that a visitor never sees the complete event. This reverses the usual pressure in web animation to make sure every movement gets noticed.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000018 -->

**Open Question — When subtlety becomes invisibility**

How much can someone miss before subtlety becomes invisibility?

### Process

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000019 -->

**Exploration — Changing relationships with AI systems**

Track how the relationship with AI systems changes as the project develops. Prompts may become shorter or more specific, and generated material may recede as the visual vocabulary becomes clearer. That evolution is part of the project too.

### Visual world

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000020 -->

**Direction — Authored layered 2D world**

Build the world as an authored 2D layered scene rather than a 3D environment or miniature video game. A diorama may be the useful model: foreground, middle ground, and distant elements such as water, structures, people, mechanical objects, light, and fog, with small movements occurring at different depths.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000021 -->

**Exploration — Illustrated theatrical diorama**

Let the visual language sit somewhere between an illustrated adventure, theatrical staging, and a physical diorama. The territory should remain broad for now rather than collapsing too early into a style guide.

<!-- idea-id: 20260819-000022 -->

**Reference — Environments that carry information**

*Machinarium* is useful as a reference for environments that carry information and objects that seem to belong where they are, not as a drawing style to reproduce.

## Tuesday, August 18, 2026

### Asset production

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000023 -->

**Experiment — Coastal relay scene**

Develop the coastal relay scene through visual ideation and environmental storytelling, using the station, traveler, causeway, and distant structure as a path. Avoid fantasy spectacle, game UI, and decorative clutter while testing whether the distant object creates curiosity without explaining itself.

### Character

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000024 -->

**Exploration — RPG archetypes through small cues**

Suggest old RPG archetypes such as mage, fighter, priest, or rogue without building literal fantasy characters. A silhouette and one object, perhaps a staff or sword, may be enough for recognition while remaining unobtrusive to anyone who misses the reference.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000025 -->

**Exploration — Recognition through a single cue**

Recognition can happen through one small cue rather than a pile of explanation. This principle may remain useful even if the RPG character idea proves unnecessary.

### Content

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000026 -->

**Exploration — Keep the portfolio material intact**

Keep the portfolio underneath the world intact: case studies, research, writing, prototypes, and evidence cannot disappear. The surrounding world should make someone more interested in seeing that material, not become an excuse to avoid it.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000027 -->

**Principle — Evidence over marketing**

Favor evidence over marketing. Research, prototypes, writing, process, and outcomes should remain more important than claims about the work.

### Interaction

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000028 -->

**Exploration — The psychology of moving through a world**

Borrow the psychology of moving through a game world without turning the portfolio into a game: arrive, orient yourself, notice something in the distance, wonder what it is, and choose to investigate.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000029 -->

**Exploration — The world as a way to encounter content**

The world is not the content. It is a way of encountering the content.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000030 -->

**Principle — Discovery over gamification**

Favor discovery over gamification. Curiosity and exploration do not require quests, achievements, inventories, experience points, collectibles, or a heads-up display.

### Navigation

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000031 -->

**Open Question — Exploration versus findability**

How far can exploration go before visitors have to work too hard to find the actual work?

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000032 -->

**Exploration — A map that relates destinations**

Use a map as a navigation idea because it places destinations in relationship to one another. A menu says there are six things to click; a map says those places are somewhere, which may be enough to create curiosity.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000033 -->

**Reference — Maps, distance, and partial revelation**

Game maps from *The Witcher 3* and *Assassin's Creed*, along with the hand-drawn Middle-earth maps associated with Christopher Tolkien, are useful references for distance, location, orientation, paths, and partial revelation. They are inspiration for navigation, not a fantasy costume to copy.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000034 -->

**Exploration — Conventional navigation alongside the map**

Keep conventional navigation even if a map becomes part of the experience. Case studies should not become difficult to find because cartography is interesting.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000035 -->

**Open Question — Primary or secondary map**

Should the map be the main navigation device or a secondary one?

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000036 -->

**Open Question — Real geography within the world**

Should the map represent a real geography inside the portfolio world?

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000037 -->

**Open Question — A map that appears when needed**

Could the map appear only at certain moments, perhaps during a transition or when the visitor needs orientation?

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000038 -->

**Open Question — How much the map should reveal**

How much information should the map reveal before exploration turns back into a menu?

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000039 -->

**Exploration — A distant object as a call to action**

A distant object may perform the same practical role as a large call-to-action button while feeling like part of the world rather than an instruction layered on top of it.

### Process

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000040 -->

**Principle — Exploration over explanation**

Favor exploration over explanation. Give visitors enough to investigate without explaining every object or relationship before curiosity has a chance to form.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000041 -->

**Principle — Restraint over density**

Favor restraint over density. The fact that something could react, move, make noise, reveal a tooltip, or become interactive is not enough reason to add it.

### Visual world

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000042 -->

**Exploration — A place rather than a stack of pages**

What if the portfolio feels like a small place someone enters and explores rather than a stack of pages? The world would surround the work without becoming the work itself.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000043 -->

**Reference — Low-Tide Signal Station and Causeway**

The Low-Tide Signal Station & Causeway is an early reference and test environment, not a locked homepage design: a small stone-and-timber station on a coastal terrace or spit, an acoustic or optical apparatus with counterweights and suspended copper cones that may lower toward or into the water, a traveler carrying something like a surveyor's staff, a stone causeway, a restrained orange marker, and a distant skeletal scaffold or wheel.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000044 -->

**Exploration — Station, traveler, causeway, and structure**

Test a spatial sequence of station, traveler, causeway, and distant structure. Its value may lie in the implied purpose and the question "What is over there?" even if the signal station itself later disappears.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000045 -->

**Exploration — Leave the world partly unexplained**

Leave the station, traveler, and distant structure partly unexplained for now. Defining what each object is, what it measures, or why it exists too quickly may remove the ambiguity that makes the place interesting.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000046 -->

**Principle — Curiosity over spectacle**

Favor curiosity over spectacle. The portfolio should not announce how imaginative it is; it should provide enough evidence for someone to become curious, then get out of the way.

<!-- idea-id: 20260818-000047 -->

**Principle — World-building over decoration**

Favor world-building over decoration. Objects and details should imply a coherent place rather than act as ornamental proof of a visual theme.
