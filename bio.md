# John Veon

As a product and design technologist in New York City, my work sits at the intersection of product strategy, systems thinking, and human-centered design, where the difficult part is rarely the interface alone. It is the larger system around it: the incentives, technical constraints, operating habits, and unanswered questions that determine whether a product can become genuinely useful.

I bring product definition, user research, interaction design, and delivery practice into the same conversation. That range has shaped a career across product and program leadership roles at Capital One, Spotify, Viacom/Paramount, and Industrial Color Software. I have worked with teams building enterprise software, digital media products, and internal platforms, moving between the strategic questions of direction and prioritization and the concrete work of mapping journeys, designing screens, and preparing a release.

At Capital One, I began as a delivery lead supporting network engineering work, including SD-WAN implementations and firewall deployments across the company’s retail footprint. I later took on product and UX leadership for an enterprise application used by branch and cafe locations. In that role, I led product definition, UX research, interface design, and feature planning for the company's internal network appliances dashboard, using Figma and Capital One’s Gravity design system. The work required a broad view of how a product behaves in the world: customer needs, technical architecture, team capacity, release cadence, and the small design decisions that shape whether a tool makes someone’s day easier or more difficult.

My approach is informed by product management, agile delivery, human-computer interaction, and UX research. I am an expert in agile methodologies, including SAFe, Scrum, and Kanban, and a Certified OKR Coach. My SAFe Program Consultant (SPC) credential is a demanding certification that qualified me to train and certify other practitioners in SAFe at Capital One. That experience sharpened my practical view of agility: teams need more than a vocabulary of ceremonies. They need the judgment to adapt their methods to the work in front of them, whether they are clarifying a product decision, working through a technical dependency, or learning from a release that did not go as planned.

For me, methods are tools rather than rituals. A roadmap, workshop, prototype, or retrospective is useful only when it helps a team see the problem more clearly and act on it with greater care.

After earning a Professional Certificate in Human-Centered Design, I decided to attend Pratt Institute full time as a graduate student. I graduated with honors, earning a Master of Science in Information Experience Design, and worked as a graduate assistant in teaching and research. The program brought together information science, human-centered design, human-computer interaction, and user experience, fields that have become central to the way I frame product work. Good systems thinking is not an abstraction for its own sake. It is a way to notice the people, dependencies, and consequences that disappear when a problem is reduced to a feature request.

That attention to people extends beyond the workplace. I am a veteran and former paratrooper with the 82nd Airborne Division. In 2015, I completed a 10,000-mile solo motorcycle ride to raise awareness of veteran suicide and raised $10,000 for Iraq and Afghanistan Veterans of America without corporate sponsorship. I also served as a founding board member of Pancakes for Roger and continue to volunteer with the organization, helping organize community fitness events and fundraising efforts.
